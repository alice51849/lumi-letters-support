Which stage is your child at?

  1. Letters and letter sounds (knows a few letters, starting to hear the first sound in a word). This is often preschool or pre-K in the US, nursery or Reception in the UK, and kinder or early Prep in Australia.
  2. Blending and decoding (sounding out c-a-t, reading simple words).
  3. Reading books (decodable readers, sight words, comprehension).

Most apps cover more than one stage. Ours covers only the first.

Where Lumi Letters falls short

  • No ratings yet in the US. Every other app here has thousands of reviews.
  • It stops before reading. It works on single letters and sounds: no blending of whole words, no decodable books and no sight words. When your child is ready to sound out whole words, you will need another app or real books.
  • Narrower than the big free apps. Khan Academy Kids and ABC Kids list no in-app purchases on their store pages and cover as much of the letter stage, or more.
  • No teacher or class accounts. Progress stays on the device.
  • We do not claim alignment with any national curriculum or phonics scheme. If your school follows a specific scheme, an app built on that scheme may fit better.
  • The free version covers only the start: the letter A and the first planet. Everything else needs the one-time in-app purchase.

Who we think should pick Lumi Letters

  • Parents of a young child who is just starting letters and wants calm, focused practice of letter names, letter sounds and neat letter formation, with no ads and no subscription.
  • Families who want one purchase for the whole family, offline play and an app that collects no data.
  • Parents and preschool teachers who like a printed sheet: Pro exports a PDF worksheet with the letters practiced that day.

Who should pick something else

  • Children already blending words: choose an app with decodable reading.
  • Families who want one app for reading and math: a broad app covers more.
  • Anyone who prefers to rely on many parent reviews: the larger apps give you that today.

A note from us

No app teaches a child to read on its own. Reading together every day matters more than any app on this list.
